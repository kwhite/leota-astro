## When last we met...

Our team of investigators were treated to Russian-style hospitality in the home of John's biological parents in the mysterious Sunken City. They also discovered the shocking origin of Sergei – he is a clone of John, augmented by Daedalus, Sergei's creator. In a bid to secure a way back home to the surface, all but Sergei traveled to the central dome of the Sunken City on a reconnaissance mission. Their goal was to assess how, and if, it's possible to help Catherine, John's mother, to take control of the Tower of Judgment –  and through the Tower, rule the Sunken City itself.

## Tower Gift Shop

![](/assets/images/2026/09/tower.webp)

> Clerk: "Do not shop from that wall over there. Those things will all turn to dust when you go home. Shop from this wall over here. And remember... we are fiction!"

Having found no way through the brass door in the library, the crew regroups in the lobby to try the gift shop – the only place left on this floor to search. It contains exactly what a gift shop should contain: golem figurines. Golem socks. The tower on kitchen towels and oven mitts. Jigsaw puzzles and art prints of the lighthouse from four different angles, and a turtle on everything that isn't a golem or the tower.

Wren uses the bathroom outside the shop on the off chance that she can find some sign of a golem within; but if they're the cleaning crew, they're a tidy one, because there's no trace of any kind of flakes, dust, leavings, anything crystalline she could scrape into a pocket and hand to Sergei. There's also no vent or maintenance hatch that might get them into the tower walls.

Maeve digs through the bin of golem keychains. Somewhere around the third handful, she notices that one of the little golems is wrong — the weight is wrong, the texture is wrong, the sheen is wrong. She spots the feel of crystal immediately, and goes to buy her treasure from the clerk.

Wren picks up an art book that talks about the tower, its construction, and its role in City life. She flips through it and doesn't make out much that's useful – history, architecture, the kind of thing sold in a gift shop. It isn't going to give away anything on purpose. London skims it. Nothing jumps out at him. Hands it to John. Nothing. Maeve takes a turn, flipping through the oversized glossy pages, and lets the pictures do the work. The crystals that decorate the tower are called Orichalcum, and it is the same substance the golems and columns are made of. Orichalcum comes in two forms, crystalline and metallic, and they repel one another. They purchase the book as well, in case it provides Sergei with useful information.

Chatting up the clerk, Maeve asks about customs, paperwork, anything required for getting a souvenir topside. Turns out... this is considered part of the same city. The clerk just cheerfully reminds them that the Sunken City is fictional. Getting out is the harder question, and the clerk has only heard stories.

There are two ways out that the clerk knows of. There are tunnels — the kind that connect to other tunnels that come up on the surface. And there is a ghost ship. The ship has no schedule. It could be here in an hour or next month.

> "It sinks, and most people die, but then it returns to the surface."

Not exactly reassuring, even if ghosts are your Thing.

The chatty clerk also tells them other colorful rumors about the Sunken City – that it's the Elders, not just the tower, that are necessary to keep it functional. And also that while the golems we see around the city all look the same, there are rumors of other types of golems kept in waiting, hidden somewhere, that nobody has ever seen.

Wren asks how they can find the famous "Amazing Lock" that nobody can open, and the clerk points the group in the right direction.

## The Locked Door

> Maeve: "So they have no staff and no assistants that come and go? They're all just up there by themselves? Seems very lonely to me." <br />Guard: "When you're several thousand years old, you have different priorities."

The famous locked door is down a short side hallway, away from the main traffic. There's a guard here who looks like a tall, broad human until the light hits him just so and you realize there's an impression of solid stone.

The lock itself is dizzyingly complicated. It takes up the entire surface of the door. Trying to examine it with a mythos viewpoint just makes it more complicated, not less; it becomes clear that you can't even start working on the door until you provide some kind of external physical substance to reveal the first stage of the lock. The group is pretty sure that's Orichalcum, but... then what?

The guard is content to let people touch the door, as long as they don't appear to be actively trying to manipulate the lock. Wren asks the guard all about the lock, and when she asks if anyone's ever tried to break in, he tells her that the last time it happened was about three years ago. The lock, of course, has its own defenses. It takes care of threats before anyone can intervene.

He doesn't know the way in, and none of them have keys. Nobody does. Nobody goes up, and nobody comes down. No maintenance, no staff, no deliveries, no nothing. Just a hundred stories of silence, and the assumption that the Elders live at the top, doing whatever it is Elders do.

After exploring this last public location in the tower, they decided to return to Rasputin's dome to meet with Sergei, rather than staying the night here in the central dome.

London puts together that these are probably the same tunnels that got them out of  Tlālōcān, the watery pocket location where they pursued Rogelio. The sewer they exited was an illogical distance from La Colonia.

## Dinner

> Catherine: "It is not expected in one day that you have solved a problem that has been plaguing us for hundreds, if not thousands of years. So I understand the need for another day. But eventually patience will run out."

It's late enough that a cuddle pile of cultists are asleep on the floor, but the kitchen staff is accommodating and gets the group whatever they want to eat. When the pavlova shows up for dessert, so does Catherine, immaculate despite the late hour.

She asks them for an update on what they found. Unfortunately, it is nothing she didn't already know, beyond a certainty that both forms of orichalcum are necessary for the first step in the puzzle of the locked door. She tells them a few "entertaining" stories about how the locked door fights back. Everything from turning people into squirrels to spraying them with acid to making them permanently invisible in an inconvenient kind of way.

She was already familiar with the blank brass door in the library as well. They say nothing about Maeve's experience phasing through it.

They share an update on Sergei's ray gun, though they don't reveal that it's complete. Just that it's a work in progress for golem suppression.

London acknowledges they'll have to go back to the city and probably stay for a few days in order to make real progress, and they'll need to take Sergei along to do it. A more exhaustive exploration of the city and its major structures is warranted, as a place like the lighthouse may give a different vantage point on the tower. Catherine is understanding, but does warn him that her patience is not infinite.

The group returns to the guest floor and congregates in Maeve's room. They use the opportunity for a quick final conference to restate their planned excursion to the central dome in "private," as they know this room is still bugged.

Then they scatter for the night.

## In the Walls

> GalleryWren: "Ooh, are y'all like watching people? Are y'all Eyes Wide Shutting?"

Still restless, London decides to explore some of these passages within the walls himself. Finding an entrance from his bathroom, he takes stairs down to the first floor passage. Covered peepholes adorn the walls looking into most of the rooms. Servants' quarters, ballrooms... John's room. No cameras, so it isn't security; it's personal entertainment.

A footfall sounds from around the corner ahead, and London tries to retreat. He trips on a stair, and decides to act confident instead of guilty. Turning to face whoever is coming, he slides his hands in his pockets and steps out of the stairwell.

It's Rasputin.

He starts walking slowly down the passage, straight for London. "You would like to come see some things that are interesting? How many very interesting things have you been able to see, my friend?"

London hedges. "I do not take your meaning."

Rasputin scoffs. "Of course you take my meaning." He gestures to the peepholes in the wall, sliding aside a few of the covers. "Some are more interesting than others. You like to see?"

"Ah. Yes. I did notice those. But no, *gracias.* I have no interest in other people's stories."

He looks London up and down. "You don't like to see? Well, then you are different than the impression I received last night from what *I* saw. What are you looking for tonight?" He continues to approach and London takes a small step backwards.

Rasputin continues. "And that is not what I heard. I have heard you are *great* at finding other people's things and selling them for good price. Well, maybe there are things I am willing to buy."

Looking for any kind of leverage, London takes a hard, mythos-powered look at what drives this man. The answer is unsettling.

> Experience. He wants the most decadent, most sensuous, most exotic experience. It is a void that he cannot fill, no matter how hard he tries. He is an Epicurean, but worse. To the point that you worry someday it will drive him to do something very different. Just to have a different experience. It's a maw of hunger.

Rasputin takes another step. "*Señor Zepeda*. Acquisitive of **many** things."

He pauses, and London takes the opportunity to start up the stairs. Then Rasputin presses forward again. "I could use someone with your... talents... on my payroll. And I pay well."

London looks back over his shoulder. "You of all people, *señor*, do not require my services."

Escaping to his room, London takes a deep breath, covers every peephole into his quarters that he can find, and exits the palace, making for the stables.

The group makes a show of packing their very few things and heading downstairs. Meanwhile, in the family wing, John leaves his room and makes for the passage. He's almost made when he passes Catherine on her way down to see the others off, but there's a handy distraction: one of the women he spent the night with the first night here. Once Catherine is out of view, John backs the girl into the servant stairway, winks, and tells her to wait right here. Then he disappears down the stairs, makes for the stable, grabs London's horse, and rides for the beach.

At the front door, the larger group says goodbye to Catherine, who sees them off with packed lunches and a bag of the cowrie shell currency typical of the Sunken City.

She meets the ghost of a young girl who is delighted to have someone to talk to. They play hide and seek, and for the rest of their stay, the girl follows Maeve around, chatting away at her and touching her hair. The sensation is like a cool breeze.

They depart, taking the road towards the main artery that leads to the central dome.

Dire leads the way to a jagged cliff, with a small track zigzagging up its face. He explains that the cliff face at a certain point is an illusion, and they can walk right through. Having pointed them to their destination, Dire disappears into the dense foliage.

Maeve ushers everyone past her, turning back to the approaching group. In the Sunken City, everyone's mythos are a bit closer to the surface. Maeve closes her eyes, opens them, and plunges Catherine and Rasputin into a nightmare, planting seeds of madness in the turmoil. In the hallucination she crafts for them, the Elders, having discovered Catherine's plan, arrive in the flesh with an army of twelve-foot crystal golems, all bathed in a glaring, blinding pure white light. She commands the ghosts in the area to add to the effect with cold winds and grasping hands.

She plunges into the tunnel behind her as the screaming starts. But the light was so bright that in the darkness, she is completely blinded as spots dance before her eyes. John gets off the horse, grabs Maeve with his inhumanly strong arms, and starts hauling her forward. London grabs the reins and remounts. Kevin tears down the tunnel, clipping a corner and cutting his arm open in the process.

Wren turns back to the opening, takes a deep breath, and breathes fire out into the woods. She incinerates the brush and foliage around the entrance to the tunnel, causing it to burn and create a barrier between the fleeing group and the guards beyond.

The horse guides them in the right direction through near-total darkness until it hits the barrier edge where the Mist changes. Both the horse and Ariki pop out of existence; London whispers goodbye to the horse before it disappears and he hits the ground.

The smell hits them even before their eyes adjust to the emergent, weak light.  The tunnels transition to a more industrial material, leaving the rough cavern walls behind.

They have arrived in the Undertrash, domain of the Queen of the Night.
