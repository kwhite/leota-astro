## When last we met…
Erica Garza has fled the people meant to protect her on her father’s behalf, and has landed in the hands of Jakub Grund. Maria, the only functional ruler of Mictlān, intends to get her back. The crew has offered to help, which leaves the other half of their problem — whatever Armand Short intends to do with the four Tezcatlipocas that will impact divine Mythos — hanging in the balance.

## Back at the Ranch
Sergei begins building the “Mythos multimeter,” a tool to investigate how items like collars and suppression bullets interfere with Rifts. The collar behaves very differently depending on who approaches or wears it. Whether those differences point to a common weakness in the connection between a person and their Mythos remains an open question.

With some help from his earlier conversation with Daedalus, Sergei makes progress. He has to break open the special bullet Maeve gave him. It remains useful for research, considerably less useful for shooting. Three live suppression rounds remain, all with Maeve, as she gathers her things and plunges into the tunnels with Cillian, Kevin, and Fiona in response to their friends’ call for aid. *(Oh, yeah, Kevin came back here with Fiona and then left again. Roll with it.)*

Sergei stays behind to work. Daniel wants to join the rescue party, but Geoffrey objects to sending him into a dangerous place given his present state of mind.

> Geoffrey: “I’m not comfortable with this. I **insist** that you stay.”

Daniel reluctantly agrees. He can help Sergei with the testing, and perhaps tilt the odds in his favor. His friends leave for the Warrens without him.

## The Isabella Effect. Again.
John has been checking his phone repeatedly, texting in furious bursts. Eventually, he looks up long enough to pass along a message to Maria.

> John: “Isabella says hi.”

She instantly recognizes the name. Isabella Aguilar knows Maria, knows the Undertrash, knows the Warrens, and is apparently sending help. She is not coming herself, and John cannot say who the help will be or when it will arrive.

This prompts John to reconsider quite a lot about his artistic collaborator. There are the benefit concerts, the resources, the underground contacts. He begins to suspect Isabella has been doing considerably more against Armand than he realized. She warns him that there’s a possibility he’ll be arrested when he (literally) surfaces, but she wants him to chance the benefit concert anyway.

The others would have appreciated hearing about this sooner, and all give John some serious side-eye. But John is only just putting it together himself. London points out that they, too, have managed to acquire Undertrash contacts in a remarkably short time, so she’s not the only one who knows these people. John concedes that they are all a little bit crazy, so this is not apples-to-apples. Except that talking about Apples is stressful right now, so he picks some other metaphor.

He knows what a celebrity can accomplish with enough staff to handle everything else. In his case, that mostly made time for sex and drugs. He suspects Isabella has been using hers for more altruistic purposes.

## The Accidental Fixer

Maria still has bloody tear tracks on her cheeks *(and awkwardly declines Maeve’s offer of a very crumpled tissue from her back pocket)* as they head into the dark.

The Warrens make the rest of the Undertrash look almost aesthetically pleasing. Elsewhere, the rubbish might be useful. Here, everything is rotting, wet, or unpleasantly organic. Bad feelings and bad smells come out of the entrance in waves. The deeper they go, the less kindness and reassurance will help them; harmful impulses and weaknesses have rather better prospects. Healing is unreliable. Violence should be *fine*.

Fiona travels with the group, having decided they need the backup. Someone needs to watch out for Cillian, apparently, whether he thinks he needs watching or not. She nods familiarly to Maria.

Before going farther, they review what they know about Grund. Cillian uses his phone to dig through police intel: Grund is wanted for murder, badly scarred, and has a repeatedly broken nose. In photos, his face looks misshapen and not quite right, and his teeth look almost sharpened. 

Wren immediately recognizes him.

> Wren: “He dropped off a Chevy Impala years ago, and there was an **elbow** in the trunk. Not an arm. Just the **elbow**.”

She called the police to deal with the stray body parts. He never came back for the car, leaving her to deal with that as well.

Maeve connects Grund to a gruesome (ghostly) cold case story she once told through her online comic, and the rescue acquires a more urgent deadline. Grund likes to play with his victims. If Erica is still alive, she is probably in a great deal of pain.

> Maeve: “Maria… how long ago did she run away?”<br />
> Maria: “Just yesterday.”<br />
> Maeve: “Yesterday? Let’s move.”

They have help coming from Isabella, but no luxury of time. Instead, they push deeper into the darkness.

## Rule of Wolves
Geoffrey tries to map their progress on his phone, but the twisting and turning of the Warrens is too complex, and his grid-based construction quickly fails him. As they try to navigate the ever-darkening tunnels, three troglodytes get the jump on the crew. One carries a spiked bat, another a sharpened piece of trash can, and the third a rock. The table immediately identifies them as Sleestacks. The MC calls them Troglodytes. *(Nerds? What nerds?)*

Wren hits one and takes a ferocious bat blow in return. John tries a rock. Their opponents have tough skin, and ordinary blows with ordinary fists and ordinary weapons are accomplishing very little.

Kevin attempts an attack with his walking stick that he does not *really* want to land. As he swings, his footwork goes wrong; he strikes the magical rifle slung across John’s back, shifting its position and discharging into an attacker’s shoulder. Whiro’s enthusiasm for destruction proves useful to the collective… this time around.

London draws his switchblade and drives it into the fresh bullet wound, opening it further. He takes a bat to the back for his trouble.

> Geoffrey: “I am calling it ‘Geoffrey’s Big Boner.’”

Geoffrey retreats a few steps, shoving up his sleeve and revealing a tattoo of a snarling wolf. He starts frantically chanting *“Big bad wolf… big bad wolf… big bad wolf…”* under his breath. The wolf manifests, powerful, aggressive, and angry. Unfortunately, it’s also totally disinterested in the distinction between Geoffrey’s friends and his enemies. It takes a swipe at John, who ducks as its claws strike sparks along the wall. Geoffrey scrambles through the rubbish looking for an alternative weapon and finds a large, somewhat gnawed bone. *(The table handles this with its usual maturity.)*

Maeve looks around for ghosts. There are plenty, including agitated spirits swirling around the attackers. She gathers four while Cillian charges the wounded enemy, shoves him away from London, and disappears before the return swing connects. Fiona and Maria are handling opponents of their own, with considerably more success than most of the crew.

Wren is also getting text messages from Marcus. His assistance initially reaches only her — a quickly texted Latin phrase that makes her feel more positive and protected. John finally admits he does not know how to use Sergei’s gun and tosses it over to her, delivering it upside down but pointed away from her. Wren corrects the orientation and shoots the wounded troglodyte attacker between the eyes.

Maeve, ghosts gathered to her, has a different plan. At Discord, possession made her strong enough to take someone down. This time… she invites it.

She sends three ghosts to pin Maria’s opponent to give her ally an advantage, and calls the fourth into herself. Maeve’s appearance shifts, her eyes going duotone again; this time, the fingers of one hand turn inky and strange, a black stain bleeding up her hand. She throws herself at Fiona’s attacker with supernatural speed and strength, slams him against the wall, cracking his head against the stone and breaking his neck.

Maeve quickly ejects the spirit, leaving her cold and shaken, as the troglodyte body slumps to the ground.

> Cillian: “Hope this works.”

Cillian reappears elsewhere in the room, then goes to Wren and touches her, turning her toward the last attacker. From her perspective, everything around them seems nearly frozen in time, while she can move and aim.

Wren shoots. 

To everyone else’s eyes, the attacker suddenly explodes without warning. Whatever Cillian is doing with time, he’s doing it faster than anyone else can process.

Geoffrey’s wolf remains a toothy problem. His attempt to direct it toward the dead lizard people for a snack earns him another attack on his person instead. He falls backward, drops the bone, and loses his concentration. The wolf dissolves into mist before anyone has to find out whether hurting it would also hurt him.

These were the underlings… and it was’t an easy fight. The tunnels are still inky and despondent, and they still have to get to Erica.

## An Ancient Mystery
With space to catch their breath, Fiona and Maria express concern at the crew’s ineffectiveness against the troglodytes. Fiona demonstrates her spectral spear and explains the advantages of hitting things hard enough to matter — which means not just using your fists. Understanding that they need to enhance their fighting abilities, the crew considers what can be made from the rubbish, weapons, and remains around them. They come up with a haphazard collection of improvised bombs and fire devices which might possibly even work correctly when used. 

Wren creates a new group text thread with Marcus, thanks him for the help, and confirms that they will catch that beer later *(saving lives is an effective way to get a date)*. Everyone then receives a Latin blessing from him which helps hold the Warrens’ effects at bay.

As they continue down the twisting passages, London detects another group of lizard people ahead of them. The crew is much more strategic this time around, having armed themselves with the weapons of their fallen opponents. This new group of troglodytes is distracted by a large, human-sized carved stone figure standing against a wall, which they are poking at. 

After the fight resolves, Wren goes to help the stone entity. She gently touches it. 

It takes a heavy swipe at her. 

Wren dodges out of the way without being hurt and, scrambling backwards, stammers an explanation that they thought it was in trouble. The figure keeps coming. It is slow moving, so the group begins to retreat further down the hallway. But as they pass it…

London and Kevin can suddenly see ghosts everywhere. London is horrified, the spectres pressing in on him. Maeve, meanwhile, is unfazed — but she finds the vaguely human monolith beautiful. Its dark magic has something to do with ghosts, and she wants to understand it. She gives it a glowing compliment, and feels warmth from it, as she experiences sudden reverence and an urge to protect it.  Its pulse of pleasure at her awestruck tone is unmistakable. 

Wren turns around, sees what is happening, and starts heading back for Maeve. John is closer and recognizes the look of someone entranced; he has seen that expression on enough groupies to know. Maria cannot snap her out of it, so John puts an arm around Maeve and tries to redirect her toward their next party — a typical late Saturday night for them.

> John: “There’s tacos at the next party, Maeve.”<br />
> Maeve: “Oh, that’s okay… I’m not hungry.”<br />
> [Entire room panics]

John uses his own undeniable charisma to counteract the pull of the strange stone monolith. Maeve manages to break free of the need to stay with his help, although she still finds the entity fascinating, and doesn’t think it is bad. 

Wren’s concern for Maeve brings her back within range of the monolith. As soon as she gets too close, a magical blast passes around Maeve and hits Wren, breaking bones. John is impacted by the blast as well, but instead of physical damage… he’s suddenly ravenous, experiencing cramps of overwhelming hunger.

> Maeve: “Don’t touch my ghosts.”<br />
> Kevin: “They weren’t your ghosts.”<br />
> Maeve: “They’re always my ghosts.”

While London is overwhelmed by the souls of the dead, Kevin also observes the now-tangible gathering of spirits, with a less visceral reaction. He can tell these ghosts did not all die together; some are from different time periods. But a substantial group of them perished in a single, terrible event. And they seem tied to the stone somehow.

<Callout color="blue" emoji="🔎"> What is this strange, stone monolith? It seems one event accounts for a group of deaths, but not all the spirits gathered here. What happened — and why they are here?</Callout>

Maeve, freed from the compulsion, politely apologizes to the monolith for their intrusion and explains that they need to leave to help someone. John bodily hauls a flailing London away from the scene. As the group reaches the end of the hallway, the stone figure slowly returns to its place standing sentinel against the wall. The Sight begins to fade.

> London: “Dios mío, how do you deal with that **all the time**?”

Far enough away from the chaos, Wren manages to heal herself, undoing the worst of the damage to her ribs and hands. Marcus’s blessing is still counteracting enough of the Warrens’ influence for her powers to work.

John, with the worst case of munchies ever, asks around for food. Maeve has been hungry for two days, and grumpily tells him there’s *nothing* to eat here. She looked before they left Yasmin’s territory. The possibility of Sleestack flambé is raised… but does not become lunch. Tired, worried, and hungry, they all continue toward the haunting cries echoing out of the dark before them.

## Fortune Favors the Foolish
Deeper in the Warrens, Marcus’s blessing is no longer quite enough to cancel out the oppressive darkness. The sobbing grows louder. Beyond the hastily dug rooms and makeshift barriers, they find a real wooden door with cracks in it. The gut-wrenching cries seem to come from the other side.

> Geoffrey [texting]: “Hey honey. We’re in a really dark spot. Anything you can do to help us out down here?”<br />
> Daniel [texting]: “Are you giving me permission to 'go cosmic?’”<br />
> Geoffrey [texting]: “Yes. Yes, please. Anything. Anything will help.”

From painfully far away, Daniel tilts fortune in their favor. **Fortune Favors the Foolish** cancels the remaining pressure of this level of the Warrens and gives his friends additional help when they do something stupid. Arguably, just *being* here qualifies.

Geoffrey thanks him while Kevin cautiously listens at the door. John hunches down, trying to peer through the cracks. Someone suggests sending a ghost… but Maeve never gets a chance.

The door slams open.

## The Face of Carnage
Jakub Grund stands in the doorway with a nasty smile. John never sees the disturbing expression, being already at crotch level, but he goes for an immediate low blow instead. Grund is less hurt than he ought to be and grabs John, wrenching him upright. Wren fires Sergei’s gun into Grund’s groin, which hurts him, but not enough to persuade him to release his human shield. If anything, his grip on John tightens.

He seems to be getting stronger with each attack against him. Fiona notices and abandons her next swing in favor of singing a few words in Gaelic, inspiring the crew.

Maeve runs straight toward Grund, becoming incorporeal just long enough to slip *through* him and into the room. She stumbles, becoming solid again prone in front of a troglodyte and rolling out of its reach as it swings at her. She takes stock of the room.

Inside, Erica is chained to a rough stone wall, shaking with tears. She is in a bloody and terrible state, having clearly been tortured for hours. Two troglodytes and another human are in the small room with her as the crew appear one by one.

London squeezes past Grund while he is distracted by Wren and Fiona, while Kevin tumbles in and narrowly avoids being stomped by a troglodyte. To an outside observer, Cillian also seems to disappear in the hallway and then reappear in the room beyond Grund, where things are getting quite crowded.

Back in the hallway, Maria gets hold of Grund’s wrists and twists until he drops John, who lands on his backside at Grund’s feet. Looking for an advantage, the crew considers fire, but the logistics of room and hallway and the position of their allies make that difficult. They need to get Erica free and keep Grund occupied long enough to clear the way.

Maeve rolls up to one knee and fires the suppression gun she picked off the Men in Gray. Her bullet strikes Grund squarely in the back before the gun jams, leaving two live suppression rounds trapped inside. Grund roars and shrinks; his face becomes more human. He is still a strong, dangerous man, but the supernatural advantages are gone.

Geoffrey creates the ultimate distraction. He moves in and kisses him.

Grund’s attention becomes very focused and very angry. He tries to backhand Geoffrey, who sidesteps with the practiced recognition of someone who has encountered this response before. Geoffrey backs down the hallway, taunting him to follow. Wren puts another shot with Sergei’s rifle into Grund’s shoulder while he is looking elsewhere.

Inside the room, London drives his jagged shard of scrap metal into the troglodyte threatening Maeve and takes a rake of the creature’s filthy claws for his trouble. A follow-up attack brings it down.

Kevin jumps onto Grund’s back, clings to him, and whispers in the man’s ear in Māori. The lights around them dim. Tattoo-like markings spread across Kevin’s face as he terrifies a man who has rarely known fear. Grund falls to his knees, clutching his head, and Kevin drops off and skitters almost unnaturally into a corner. The markings on his skin vanish; the lights return.

## Cleaning House
Focused on Erica, Maeve sends a ghost to find the key to her chains. This room has more than its share of spirits, and one of the dead has been forced to watch Grund torture and kill his victims long enough to know exactly which drawer holds it.

Fiona and Cillian finish the remaining troglodyte together, with the coordination of people who’ve fought as a team before. Maria prevents the human from joining the fight with a stern talking-to. London speaks softly to Erica in Spanish while Maeve retrieves the key, and they work together to get her free, unaware of what’s happening outside.

Out in the hallway, the others give Grund some space as he writhes in the throes of the nightmare Kevin has induced. Wren throws alcohol over him and calls for fire. Geoffrey’s spray-paint flamethrower supplies it. Burned and blackened, Grund collapses. Even unconscious, he twitches and jerks, caught in Kevin’s powerful magic. Yet he still lives. 

The crew does not feel particularly good about it, but they do not want Grund coming back for them. Ultimately, Wren and John go back and forth on what to do, and John eventually crushes Grund’s head in with the spiked bat he wields.

Kevin is still mumbling in the corner. Geoffrey goes to comfort him, trying to remind him that he is here, and Kevin is not alone. He keeps repeating something Geoffrey can’t figure out: *“Ehara rātou i ō wairua.”* Then he lurches to his feet, pushing past Geoffrey to find Maeve in the other room. He looks at her, the whites of his eyes visible. “They are not. Your. Ghosts.” He turns and walks away.

Maeve and London get Erica free of her shackles and gently assist her up, supporting her weight between them. It is still too much pain, and she promptly passes out. Wren asks whether there is anything she can do, but it’s clear that her healing won’t be enough; Erica needs real medical attention, and she needs it quickly. 

Marcus and Fiona’s blessings begin to fade. Even with Grund gone, the dark power this deep in the Warrens starts pressing in on them all like a physical presence. 

And they have a long, long way to go to get back out.
