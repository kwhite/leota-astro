## When last we met…
After a lot of reviewing what they knew, the crew realized that Armand’s mythos is the conquistador, Hernán Cortéz, and that he plans to use the four Tezcatlipocas in some kind of ritual that will negatively affect divine Rifts across the city. Following this realization, the crew split in two halves. Daniel, Sergei, Cillian, and Maeve stayed in the lair of the Queen of the Night to research ways to insulate divine rifts from whatever nefarious thing Armand has planned. 

Meanwhile, Wren, John, Geoffrey, and London *(and Kevin, Sir-Not-Appearing-In-This-Episode)* made their way through the tunnels of the Undertrash toward the entrance to Mictlān, the Aztec underworld. Their goal is to speak with Maria and Miguel, the keepers of Mictlān, about Armand Short and the Tezcatlipocas, to figure out what he has planned and how to stop it. After facing off against an undead creature, they find their path blocked by deliberately collapsed rubble as they debate which route to take next.

## The Road to Mictlān
![](/assets/images/2026/10/marcus2.webp)

In the tunnels, the Mictlān crew examines the destruction blocking their path. The stones are immovable. As they contemplate doubling back, London suddenly goes still. 

They are not alone.

A figure emerges from the shadows. Clean-cut, impeccably dressed, guns low on his hips, hands high in the air. In a charming drawl, he introduces himself as Marcus Romsia. He and his “boys” have been following the group for a while. He saw them dealing with the “vampire,” and he’s impressed with their fighting skills, especially Wren’s. He delivers *that* individual compliment with an irresistible smile. Which Wren emphatically resists.

From their conversations with Yasmin, the crew knows Marcus is one of the Undertrash's competing powers. He's armed, accompanied by his people, and curious about the strangers wandering through his territory. He’s also aware of a _real_ way into Mictlān, and he’s willing to share… in exchange for information about why they’re headed there in the first place. Mictlān isn’t a picnic. People don’t come back out.

They agree to his price, and the group starts walking. While Geoffrey assures Marcus that they have no interest in local politics, London tries to take a closer look at Marcus’s powers. He gets the impression of a statue, a man fighting a bull. Clearly Roman, and it rings some bells… maybe mystery cult symbolism? But he can’t place it — classical antiquity isn’t his strongest artistic period. He also tries to get a read on what Marcus wants more than anything else. The answer is quite simple — his own place, his own territory, his own place to belong.

Still walking, London begins offering Marcus carefully selected pieces of the truth. He's looking for information about Tezcatlipoca, specifically the deity’s four aspects. Marcus knows enough about La Colonia to recognize where this might be going and begins to ask whether London's interest has something to do with Tez's disappearance.

London acknowledges that it does. Understanding the combined mythology might help them figure out where Tez has gone. Marcus wants to know whether they're planning to rescue him, and that’s where the conversation becomes uncomfortable.

Marcus was once one of Tez's trusted associates, until their relationship ended badly enough to drive him out of La Colonia. He has no interest in helping Tez, and even less interest in helping someone else restore him to power.

London tries to steer the conversation away from that possibility without revealing what the crew actually knows. Everything he says is true, but he's being extremely careful about which truths he shares. Marcus notices, and he isn’t pleased. He keeps asking questions, and London keeps giving him answers that don't quite explain why they're going to Mictlān. Eventually, Marcus turns to the others, hoping someone will be more forthcoming.

John tries offering stories about the Sunken City, but Marcus is laser-focused on right here, right now. By the time they reach an abandoned subway station, he's had enough. He stops the group and makes it clear that they're not going any farther until someone tells him what's actually happening. Geoffrey reiterates that they're not interested in politics. Marcus isn't satisfied with negation — he wants answers.

Wren attempts to help, breaking into the uneasy conversation. Unfortunately, Wren has misunderstood part of the plan. She thinks they're going into Mictlān to protect something and offers a somewhat tangled explanation involving a breaker and ensuring it's properly insulated.

Marcus has absolutely no idea what she's talking about.

To be fair, Wren doesn't entirely know what she's talking about, either. She's working from what she believes the crew is trying to accomplish, which isn't quite what the others have in mind.
Marcus nevertheless finds her delightful. But he still isn’t budging, and they can’t do this without him. 

> Marcus: "I will tell you what I know of Mictlān, but I'm warning you in advance, it ain't much. And it ain't good.”

London finally gives up more information. Still not the whole truth, but enough to satisfy. He tells Marcus that they believe Armand Short has Tez, Águila de Plata, and their friend Adriana — three of the Tezcatlipocas. Erica is missing as well, although they don't believe she's being held with the others — her disappearance is likely connected to a backup plan by Tez. Adriana matters to London personally, and finding her is his immediate priority. He’d like to find Erica too, just to make sure Armand doesn’t. Because whatever Armand is planning will exploit the four of them and won’t be good for anyone. Finding Mictlān, Maria, and Miguel is a desperate bid for information from the Aztec underworld about the intricacies of their creator/destroyer gods, so they can hopefully figure out what Armand has planned, how to stop it, and how to rescue their friend.

Marcus eventually relents, hooking up carts sitting on the old subway tracks as he continues to try his luck with Wren. The conversation ebbs as the carts thunder down the tracks until they slow at another, smaller subway station.

## The Enemy of my Enemy
Exiting the new station, the group resumes walking. With the immediate disagreement settled, London takes the opportunity to ask what Marcus knows about the situation in La Colonia.
Apparently, things are going about as well as might be expected when a powerful crime boss disappears without leaving anyone firmly in charge. Alma, the Jaguar, and the other sicarios have been trying to hold Tez's territory against his enemies, but Marcus believes they're already beginning to turn on one another. 

The organization is coming apart, and Marcus admits to taking some satisfaction in watching it happen. He doesn't have much affection left for Tez. London understands and feels the same, acknowledging that this is common history between them (albeit at very different levels of influence), and Marcus’s name is quite familiar to him. 

Marcus isn't entirely enthusiastic about the chaos, though. He still has family in La Colonia, and he'd rather the district didn't descend into riots. His mother still lives there, though he does not. Another point the two share. This is rather more in common than either one is probably comfortable discovering.

Marcus may enjoy watching Tez's organization fall apart, but he doesn't want ordinary people caught in the aftermath. London has similar concerns, even if his feelings about Tez and La Colonia are considerably more complicated than he's inclined to explain. Geoffrey has a stake in the district's future as well, since he works there. Whatever happens to Tez's organization, the consequences won’t be limited to those who worked for him.

## Waiting
Marcus eventually leads the group to a concealed entrance to Mictlān. Based on his sources, both Maria and Miguel are inside. Marcus warns them that approaching the rulers of the underworld together may not be wise. He's heard that Miguel has become increasingly unstable, although he admits that most of what he knows is rumor. But one thing is for sure: Miguel is both mercurial and extremely powerful. Catching him on a bad day is a death sentence, and that’s if the obstacles you face getting into Mictlān don’t kill you first.

Maria still ventures outside Mictlān and may be easier to approach. She was a police officer before her awakening, and Marcus suspects she retains some of her former sense of duty.
The crew considers its options. They could enter Mictlān and risk confronting both rulers. Or they could wait for Maria to emerge and either talk to her directly or hide from her, then try to reach Miguel when he’s alone. 

> Geoffrey: “I vote for hiding from the cop.”<br />
> London: “It wouldn't be the first time.”

They decide to wait.

Marcus arranges for them to be notified when Maria emerges, leaving the crew with some time to kill. Wren, who has already attracted Marcus's attention, strikes up another conversation. This time, she's interested in his tattoos.

Marcus has quite a collection, including bulls, Latin lettering, and designs associated with his former life in La Colonia. Wren asks about the imagery, wondering at one point whether he might have been a bullfighter in a previous life.

Marcus seems entertained by the suggestion. He talks about the symbolism of the bull, sacrifice, and the traditions that inspired his tattoos, as well as the challenges of growing up as a white kid in La Colonia. Wren keeps asking questions, drawing out references to older mythology, including Sumerian and Persian influences. At one point, she offers Marcus her business card, and Marcus is certain he’ll need some automotive maintenance sometime soon. 

> Geoffrey: “Wren, I think his undercarriage needs a little attention.”<br />
> London: “Geoffrey, you are not helping.”<br />
> Geoffrey: “That was not intended to help.”

She's careful not to reveal much about herself, but Marcus is plainly enjoying the attention.
London is happy to let her handle him as he listens to the conversation. He turns over what he knows about Marcus and his Mythos, adding bits of information Wren uncovers to the mix. Doing some research on his phone, he puts the pieces together. The bull, the Roman setting, and the Sumerian influences eventually lead him to Mithraism, the Roman mystery cult associated with Mithras, the Roman variation on the ancient Persian sun god. He texts Wren, John, and Geoffrey with his realization, since they can’t exactly discuss it in front of Marcus. 

<Callout color="blue" emoji="🔎">
Marcus Romsia is the Rift of Mithras. Mithra is an ancient Iranian deity of covenants, light, oaths, justice, the Sun, contracts, and friendship.
</Callout>

The time passes slowly. Geoffrey works on his tattoo rendering of the vampire they fought. London reads, off down a Greco-Roman art history rabbit hole. John gets a flurry of texts and eventually steps out of earshot to make a call. 

They continue to sit. And wait.

## I have a theory…

> Maeve: "I am 100% convinced that Hozier has some kind of crazy hitchhiker thing.”<br />
> Cillian: “Hozier has a Mythos.”<br />
> Maeve: “100%. He’s got to. Oh, look! It’s an Irish one, like you! At least, Reddit thinks so.”<br />
> Cillian: “Hozier is Irish. I am Irish. I am Hozier. That’s the conclusion. What is this hooch they make down here?”

Elsewhere in the Undertrash, Maeve, Cillian, Daniel, and Sergei are trying to solve a problem with few obvious starting points. How do you protect a Rift from having its Mythos suppressed, extracted, or controlled?

### Daniel
The crew has encountered several methods of interfering with supernatural power, but they don't know exactly how Armand's ritual works, which makes developing a defense a bit of a challenge.
Daniel begins looking for commonalities among the phenomena they've already seen. 

* The Candy Witch could extract Mythos and bottle it. 
* The Men in Gray have their own methods of separating people from their supernatural identities, and ways of using supernatural gifts without “sharing space” with them. 
* The crew has encountered both smoke/mist and bullets that suppress or inhibit supernatural abilities.
* There are also the collars, which, when placed on Kevin, sapped his free will, but not his powers.

Are these different methods of disrupting the same connection between a person and their Mythos? Or are they attacking entirely different things?

### Maeve & Cillian

> Maeve: “I bet he’s a fae of some kind.”<br />
> Cillian: “Orpheus. Just because he's Irish doesn't mean he has to have an Irish god.”<br />
> Maeve: “I wouldn't know **anything** about **that**.”<br />

Maeve approaches the problem from another direction — after being extensively distracted and pulling Cillian into an animated debate about Hozier, Florence Welch, their hypothetical offspring, pointy fae ears, and Reddit.

Her take on their problem is story and symbolism. Mythology is full of elixirs and objects associated with healing, immortality, and the endowment and restoration of divine power. Perhaps one of those traditions might offer something useful that they could build on.

> Cillian: “When did you die? Was I there?”<br />
> Maeve: “Yes, you were there. Thanks for noticing.”

Cillian is still concerned that this ritual is going to end up with them dead in some spectacular way if they can’t figure out how to stop it or negate the effect. Maeve also feels the urgency — she doesn't particularly want to die, either. It wasn't much fun the first time.

Cillian seems confused, eventually remembering that Maeve drowned, an event in which he was rather directly involved. Maeve is delighted to discover what a profound emotional impact her death apparently had on him.

> Cillian: “I try to forget when my girlfriends die. It's, you know, easier that way.”<br />
> Daniel: “…Girlfriend?”<br />
> Cillian: “…Women I'm dating.”<br />
> Maeve: “Wait, does that happen frequently?”<br />

Cillian keeps talking when he probably should have stopped. *(And has now managed to raise several questions that probably deserve answers at some point.)*

### Sergei
Sergei decides to make use of the magical telephone they acquired aboard the submarine. He calls Daedalus, who is delighted to hear from his “son.” Sergei explains that they've uncovered a plot that could threaten Rifts across the City. Daedalus is immediately concerned about whether Sergei himself is in danger. Sergei admits that he might be, and Daedalus promises to help.

> Sergei: “I am very glad for this informational exchange.”

The conversation takes a few detours. Daedalus remembers Armand Short as the man behind a company selling remarkably inexpensive produce, but he's unaware of the slave labor involved. Learning that Armand is the Rift of Hernán Cortés prompts a tangent about another conquistador, Handsome John, and a possible connection to Fort Independence.
Sergei does his best to steer the conversation back toward the actual problem.

> Daedalus: “Oh, that is a lot. You have not been gone long.”<br />
> Sergei: “It's been **very** busy.”

When Sergei explains that Armand may be planning to control the City's divine Rifts, Daedalus initially points out that Sergei isn't a god and might therefore be safe, but Sergei insists that these things tend to escalate. Daedalus agrees, and offers to investigate from his end. He's astonished by how much the crew has uncovered since Sergei left.

Daedalus suggests they collaborate on the research, perhaps using FaceTime so they can show each other what they're working on. He's heard of the technology, although he doesn't seem entirely familiar with it. Sergei agrees, and Daedalus is clearly pleased at the prospect of working on a project together.

But as the conversation winds down, it becomes apparent that Daedalus is interested in rather more than a research partnership.

>Daedalus: “I care about your future, not just if you have babies, so just remember that.”

He admits that he wasn't certain Sergei would call again after returning to the surface. He asks about Sergei's work, and Sergei confesses that he’s nervous that after being gone so long he’ll be ousted. Daedalus reassures him that his employers surely won't mind his absence, and begins contemplating Sergei’s probable future. Daedalus would like to attend Sergei's wedding, should he ever marry. He'd also like to meet any children Sergei might have, which makes him wonder aloud whether Sergei is even capable of having them.

Sergei …hasn't given the matter much thought. And Daedalus realizes he's getting ahead of himself.

> Daedalus: “Please call me again.”

It's an awkward, rambling conversation, full of unsolicited scientific speculation and the occasional historical tangent. But Daedalus is plainly trying to find a place in Sergei's life, and he's worried that Sergei might not want him there. Sergei ends the call, somewhat overwhelmed.

> Sergei: “Wow. That just happened.”

### Maeve & Cillian
Maeve continues her investigation into mythological substances and tools. Ambrosia is an obvious possibility, as are legendary cauldrons, grails, sacred waters, and other objects associated with healing or immortality.

“Ambrosia” makes Cillian pause; he recognizes that name for a different reason. It’s a notorious drug circulating in Ocean Heights, where Hades operates. The police have managed to obtain samples, but the substance doesn't appear to *do* anything. It's also unusually localized, almost exclusively found in that area. Cillian’s been concerned about Ocean Heights for a long time. Before discovering that magic was real, he suspected something like human trafficking. Now he's beginning to wonder whether whatever is happening there is considerably more sinister. 

> Cillian: “Oh… like Hades might be **really** Hades?”<br />
> Maeve: “Yes. I think Hades is **really** Hades.”

Maeve has her own theory. During her first conversation with Cyrus and Seamus, they implied that Hades exercises some form of control over the residents of Ocean Heights. Cillian didn’t catch any of the subtext of that conversation, but Maeve has been thinking about it ever since.
She suspects the control might be metaphysical, and now wonders whether Ambrosia is part of the mechanism. The drug's apparent lack of effect becomes more interesting if its purpose isn't recreational.

<Callout color="blue" emoji="🔎">
    Ambrosia is a drug with no obvious effect that circulates almost exclusively in Ocean Heights, where Maeve suspects Hades exercises supernatural control over the residents. Connected? Maybe?
</Callout>

She explains that Hades being the *real* Hades is why she was asking Cillian about him in the first place. She *(or maybe Melinoë)* is particularly interested in the possibility that the Greek lord of the dead is operating in the City. Supernatural family connections and all.

Cillian mentions that Hades was once married, but his wife disappeared. Maeve raises an eyebrow, saying, “Let me guess, was it a Persephone?” But he has no idea. 

Hesitantly, Cillian asks Maeve to *tell him* if she intends to investigate Ocean Heights. He wants them to go together if it has to happen. Maeve cheekily reminds him that she's already promised not to go alone. He doesn't entirely believe her. *(Which… fair.)* 

In a more serious tone, she shares that in that same conversation with Cyrus and Seamus, they warned her that visiting Ocean Heights might be particularly dangerous and overwhelming for someone with her connection to ghosts. She suspects Hades' shades are involved.

But this is all a puzzle for another day. They go back to their research on the immediate problem of not dying.

### The Scientific Method
The collar is one of the few pieces of physical evidence they have, so Daniel decides to do some tests with it. They know how it affected Kevin, who has a divine Mythos, but they want to know what the effects are on people with different types of abilities. 

> Maeve: “Hey Daniel, hop on one leg.”<br />
> Daniel: “That would cause me to put more strain on one knee than the other. I don't think that's a good idea.”

There’s only one way to find out how it affects a folkloric Mythos: Daniel tries on the collar. It doesn’t seem to have any effect on his free will, and his powers still function.

Ultimately, Maeve and Cillian can’t touch the thing, so Sergei has to get it off of him. The collar feels sick and wrong and horrible in his hands, and he feels almost ill when he touches it. But he’s able to remove it from Daniel’s neck. 

> Maeve: “No, no, no, no, no, no, no, no!”

Just to show everyone what happens, Maeve reaches out a finger towards the collar, and it immediately strikes at her with the speed of a snake, animating of its own accord. She squeaks and starts running away from it. The collar chases her across the room, while Cillian picks up a stick and prepares to launch the thing over the proverbial fences before Sergei grabs it again. Cillian also reaches toward the collar, and it moves to entangle him as well. The collars seem to recognize divine Mythos, becoming aggressive and almost autonomous in their presence.

Inspired, Sergei decides to try building a tool to determine what’s happening. Using materials available in the Undertrash, he begins developing a diagnostic device to measure supernatural interference. A “Mythos Multimeter,” if you will. It will take him a bit to build it, and it’s not a solution in and of itself. But it’ll make these experiments in Mythic manipulation and suppression more informative moving forward.

# The Queen of Mictlān
![](/assets/images/2026/10/maria.webp)

Maria finally emerges from the concealed entrance to Mictlān, so preoccupied that she nearly walks past the strangers waiting for her. She's crying, and when the crew gets a better look, they realize the tears running down her face are blood.

London steps forward to greet her, with John close behind. Maria is startled, then immediately suspicious. She has no patience for lies, particularly from strangers who have apparently been lurking outside her home.

> John: “I think Armand Short is taking gods captive.”

London apologizes for the intrusion and explains that they've been looking for her. John decides there's no point being subtle and launches immediately into the situation. They explain what they know about Armand, the missing deities, and the four creator gods. Maria recognizes Armand's name, although she doesn't know what he's planning. She does, however, know something about Tez and his daughter.

Maria and Miguel owe Tez a favor for something that happened years ago. She won't interfere with police business on his behalf, but she agreed to protect Erica if anything happened to him. Erica wants to get away from her father's criminal world, something Maria understands and supports. Helping her escape seemed a reasonable compromise.

Unfortunately, the arrangement has gone badly wrong.

Erica made it safely into the Undertrash, where Maria and Miguel were supposed to look after her, but Jakub Grund and his men intercepted her. Maria knows she's been taken, but hasn't been able to get her back. She's been trying to persuade Miguel to help, without success. Erica is being held in the Warrens, Jakub's territory deep in the Undertrash.

Maria is visibly shaken. She made a promise to protect a young woman who wanted nothing more than to escape her father's world, and now Erica has fallen into something considerably worse. Maria is horrified that a girl trying to escape a bad situation has ended up in the hands of someone like Jakub. She intends to rescue her, with or without Miguel.

London and John assure her they'll assist her. Maria is desperate enough to accept, though she notices that the two strangers seem to have acquired several companions as Wren and Geoffrey reluctantly reveal themselves. Marcus and his men, meanwhile, have quietly disappeared.

> Maria: “For many years now I've had a voice. There is a voice, there is something inside of me that is giving me a mission that is bigger than myself, and I believe in the mission, but the mission has not taken me over. I feel it has taken over my husband.”

London asks whether Miguel might be helped, and Maria's distress takes on another dimension. She and her husband have spent years working toward a purpose tied to Mictlān, something neither of them entirely chose. Maria understands what it means to have a supernatural mission, but she's increasingly afraid that Miguel no longer remembers there's anything else. He is consumed by the problems of Mictlān. Even Erica's disappearance can't draw him away, and Maria doesn't know how to reach him anymore.

London asks whether some kind of healing that would clear his mind might be effective. Maria isn't sure. If she can't get through to him, she doesn't know who can. For now, she has a more immediate problem.

> London: “Let us find Erica. And then perhaps we can help.”<br />
> Maria: “One problem at a time. If we can find Erica, that is all I ask of you. The rest of the problems are my own.”

### Calling the Cavalry
Rescuing Erica means going into the Warrens, which is something the crew had been hoping to avoid. London points out that they should probably bring Maeve and Cillian, and Maria agrees that additional help would be welcome. Geoffrey rolls his eyes and insists that Daniel is needed as well, which London doesn’t grace with a response. Jakub has followers known as troglodytes, and Maria doesn't expect them to make the rescue easy.

London begins texting Maeve and Cillian. Geoffrey makes sure Daniel gets the message, too.
Back at Yasmin's lair, the research crew starts gathering its things in a hurry. Their investigation into Mythos suppression hasn't produced a solution to Armand's ritual, but Maeve has acquired something that might be useful in a fight.

Her original handgun, the *Extra Shot*, was taken from her back at the barn. She's since helped herself to a replacement from one of the Men in Gray, although this weapon doesn't fire ordinary ammunition — they learned that during the fight back at the snake mound. Its bullets suppress supernatural abilities rather than inflict physical damage, making it potentially very useful against a powerful Rift.

There are four rounds in the gun. Maeve unloads it and hands one to Sergei, hoping he can study the ammunition and perhaps figure out how to reproduce it. That leaves her with three shots, so she'll need to make them count.

The new gun also needs a name. Given that Maeve's old handgun was the *Extra Shot*, someone suggests an appropriately less-caffeinated alternative.

*The Decaf.*
Maeve approves.

## Into The Warrens
> Maria: “My husband is not a monster. My husband is obsessed. It is different.”

As Maria leads the others toward Jakub's territory, they ask what they're getting into.
The Warrens are a network of tunnels deep beneath the Undertrash, inhabited by Jakub and his followers. Maria calls Jakub a monster, and when someone asks whether she means he's become one in the same way Miguel has changed, she immediately objects. Jakub was already a terrible man by the time he arrived in the Undertrash. Maria believes he's made the place worse, and that the place has made him worse in return.

She tries to explain that the Undertrash isn't like the City above. The land itself seems to have feelings, and different places exert different influences on the people who enter them. The Serpent Mound feels peaceful to her, although she suspects it has something more to communicate. Mictlān has its own problems, but she believes they can be addressed in time.

The Warrens are another matter.

Jakub's hatred has infected the territory, attracting people who are already cruel, bitter, or damaged and encouraging their worst impulses. His followers, the troglodytes, have become part of that corruption. Maria warns that simply being there can make a person worse. Even some members of their own group may have… proclivities… that could become significantly more troublesome in a place like this.

<Callout color="blue" emoji="🔎">
The deeper someone travels into The Warrens, the more their weaknesses become strengths and their strengths become weaknesses. Anger, fear, and aggression can become powerful assets, while abilities rooted in hope, joy, or protection may be diminished or unavailable.
</Callout>

Maria knows what she's walking into. She also knows Erica is somewhere inside, and she has no intention of abandoning her. The others have agreed to help, and the rest of the crew is on its way.
