## When last we met…
Reunited but now in hiding, the crew explored the Undertrash and stumbled upon a serpent-shaped mound surrounded by makeshift dwellings. The eerie but idyllic setting was rudely interrupted by the sudden appearance of the Men in Gray emerging from a cloud of billowing smoke inside one of the tents. A fight ensued, which heralded the arrival of the Queen of the Night, who proceeded to chase the remaining Men in Gray across the serpent-shaped mound with her shadowy scimitars.

## After the Fight

> **John:** “You *don't* look comfortable here.”

The fighting has stopped. Most of the Men in Gray have escaped through portals, with the Queen of the Night pursuing the stragglers across the serpent-shaped mound. The crew still has one prisoner, and he would very much like to leave before she comes back.

Geoffrey stays close to Daniel, who is still disoriented from the suppressing smoke. Their prisoner insists that the raids are meant to protect the people living here by getting them out of the vicinity of the mound. The serpentine hill is some kind of artifact. They don't know exactly what it does, but they are confident it’s dangerous. And they don't think the residents should become an experiment in finding out how it works.

The crew isn't convinced that being ancient automatically makes something dangerous. But are they willing to risk people's lives to find out?

London just shrugs. Sergei pipes up from the back with a “Yeah, maybe.”

The prisoner maintains that he hasn't hurt anyone. If they leave him for the fierce woman with the scimitars, his blood is on their hands. His name is Ethan Marx. He produces an official-looking badge, complete with his name and conspicuously lacking the name of an organization. Geoffrey objects to releasing someone who can report their whereabouts to the other Men in Gray. London supplies the condition: if Ethan does that, his life is forfeit.

They decide to let him go.

> **Geoffrey:** “Okay, fuck right off then.”

Ethan scuttles into the tent, reassuring Geoffrey that the suppression Daniel is experiencing isn't permanent. Geoffrey peeks inside in time to see him disappear through the back wall. Maeve's poltergeist, still following instructions, throws something after him. 

London sees letting him go as strategic; there’s potential in having one of the Men in Gray owe them a favor. Even with his powers suppressed, he can tell them that much.

## The Benefit of the Doubt

Yasmin Farhat approaches with two scimitars that seem made of smoke or fog, their shapes stretching and reforming as they score the mound. Up close, the Queen of the Night is surprisingly small and young. The force of her presence has been doing a lot of the height work.

Daniel collects himself enough to introduce himself and the family connection: he and Geoffrey are parents to Sofia Sandoval's niece, Inez. Sofia said Yasmin could get a message to her.

She asks them what sort of message they want delivered. They throw everything at her: The Niners are clones. The Men in Gray are raiding the Undertrash and assaulting citizens. They can open portals down here. And that Geoffrey, Daniel, and the crew is temporarily taking refuge below the streets.

> **Yasmin:** “So you can fight, but you can't count.”

Yasmin asks how many attackers they fought off. Counting proves briefly difficult, and Daniel explains the lungful of smoke. Wren offers to help, rests her hands on his shoulders, and lights up with Egyptian hieroglyphics. Her healing works; Daniel feels considerably better. Yasmin notices, and gives a small smile.

Taking refuge is an option, but Yasmin wants to know whom they plan to stay with. There seems to be a wrong answer to this question, although the crew has no idea what it is. Daniel assures her that they represent themselves, have no designs on her authority, and are new to the politics down here. She asks them what they know about Jacob and Marcus. 

Nothing useful, as it turns out. John searches his extensive acquaintance list. London tries to place the names and gets nowhere. Their ignorance appears to be acceptable, and Yasmin offers them somewhere to lay low, then turns toward a cave and gestures for them to follow her. She likes stories. She would like to hear theirs.

As they walk, John volunteers that he is from the Sunken City, prompting a whispered reminder that they aren't supposed to tell people about it. Yasmin smiles and reminds them all that the place is a myth. John persists, explaining that Rasputin is his father, and at some point his memory was wiped, and he still doesn't understand how.

In some religions, Yasmin observes, memory is wiped when a person dies.

Maeve, however, remembers everything from before she died on the way down to the City. Death is part of her everyday experience, so she hadn't put much weight on the distinction. Yasmin notes that she didn't stay dead, then looks just over Maeve’s shoulder and around her person as though examining something the others can't see. Daniel asks what it is, and Yasmin tells them that she can see Maeve's death. 

She keeps walking, following a twisting, turning path through the underground.

Kevin has been following quietly. Without Whiro, he feels strangely cold. Geoffrey puts an arm around him and checks his temperature, but he feels normal to the touch.

> **Kevin:** “No, man, I feel cold on the *inside*.”

Geoffrey produces a hip flask and offers it to Kevin. Yasmin says the effects of the smoke will wear off. The conversation turns to accepting what happens to you, and whether that leaves you better off. Where Whiro is concerned, Kevin isn't sure he *wants* to be better off.

Cillian, meanwhile, is making small movements with his hands as they pass turns. He is trying to remember the route they’re taking. Geoffrey recognizes the behavior and cautions him to keep it discreet. Everyone else is largely following the mysterious woman through the tunnels without tracking where they are going. Such trusting souls.

Geoffrey proposes an arrangement to Yasmin: information on the activities of the Men in Gray and assistance with whatever she needs, in exchange for temporary shelter and message passing. Yasmin agrees. Sofia's approval lends them the benefit of the doubt… but it does not give them Yasmin’s blind trust. If information gets out that harms her people, the Undertrash is large, and there are many of them. It will go poorly for the crew if they cross her.

Geoffrey tries to explain how little they understand about the larger politics of the City. Yasmin finds this difficult to reconcile with a group that has found the supposedly mythical Sunken City, fought the Shadowstalkers, and befriended Sofia.

The crew's defense amounts to being “exceptionally gifted dumb fucks.” Much of what they have learned and what’s happened to them has been completely by accident.

Yasmin arches an eyebrow at them. She has heard people play dumb before, and that’s what this sounds like to her.

## The Serpent Realm
> GalleryGeoffrey: "System message: Scolopendra stirs!"

“Shadowstalkers” is a new name for the people the crew calls Men in Gray. They offer what they have learned about the organization's divisions (Shrouders and Rupturers), its extraction of powers, and its cooperation with the Niners in Independence. Yasmin appreciates any information they can share.

The incursions down here concentrate on the Serpent Realm. The raiders believe the serpent has power, and Yasmin agrees with them.

> **Yasmin:** “It is as ancient as the city. Once it did something, no one knows what it was.”

Maeve asks whether there is a way inside, or whether it is simply an earthen mound. It reminds her of a burial barrow. Yasmin thinks the mound itself is the sleeping Thing. But when or whether it will awaken remains uncertain.

<Callout color="blue" emoji="🔎">
The serpent remains unresolved. The Men in Gray are targeting it, its former purpose is unknown, and Yasmin speaks of an eventual awakening.

Wren offers a more immediate, tangible form of assistance. Does Yasmin have anything mechanical that needs fixing? She says there are always things to fix, and Wren has the sense they’ll revisit this later.

Another turn brings them into a honeycomb of tunnels and improvised doors, with homes tucked into the walls. Laughter comes from inside. Residents emerge, see Yasmin, curtsy, and step aside. Farther along, people smile and hand her flowers. She accepts them politely and keeps moving. While she’s cordial, she is distant. A dark cloud passing through a fairly happy village.

London already knows her Mythos is Ereshkigal, the Sumerian goddess of the dead. His knowledge of the art and history provides some additional context: Ereshkigal has it rough. She has an underworld role she doesn't enjoy, two difficult husbands — Nergal, a bully of a war god, and Tamuz, the god of spring, who is also married to her sister &mdash; a womanizer and a jackass who routinely breaks her heart. She also has a considerably more famous sister, Ishtar/Inanna. Ereshkigal's story makes Persephone's arrangement look appealing. 

It does not necessarily tell them Yasmin's life story, but it gives London an idea of her probable outlook on life. He encourages Maeve to talk to her. She shrugs and doesn’t start small, asking Yasmin about her perspective on death. It happens to everyone, Yasmin says, and gives people a reason to do good while they are alive. The dead no longer control their destiny.

Maeve argues that they don’t control their destiny, but they do still have stories. She tries to hold space for those stories—to witness them and tell them. She thinks there may be peace in that for the people who can no longer tell their own. Yasmin says it’s a kindness, though Maeve thinks she’s just doing her job.

> **Yasmin:** “The dead feed on the scraps that we provide them.”

Yasmin looks behind Maeve, glancing at Cillian. "My main concern is that your spring god is nicer than ours."

Maeve asks whether Yasmin has trusted people who support her in her rule. She says there are a few, but not many.  Ultimately, she does this job because someone must. No one expects it of her, but that doesn’t change the necessity of someone stepping up to do it. 

## The Throne Room

Beyond the throne room, another door opens. A red-haired woman strolls in confidently. 

“You know, I heard there was some adventure that you had without me.”

Yasmin shoots her a glare. “We could have used your help, Fiona.”

She looks unrepentant. “Sorry I missed it. Are these the others that were with you when it happened?”

Yasmin leans against the doorframe. “Yes. They were more helpful than you.”

The woman has a hardened, piratical air. There’s familiarity here, and Yasmin seems more relaxed and animated around her.

Cillian takes one shocked look at the woman and blurts out, “Fiona?!?”

Ever curious, Maeve asks how they know each other. Fiona tells her that she sometimes works for his mother.

Wait… his *mother*?

Cillian would prefer not to do this and tries to shut the conversation down. Maeve points out that he has options. She smirks at him, half playful, half serious. “You do realize that at some point you can either tell me things, or I can go find things out. One of those options lets you control the narrative.”

Fiona cracks up. Cillian is glaring at her as she explains that Cillian's mother currently wants to kill her. Why? Lots of reasons. But none she is willing to share.

London's powers are beginning to return, and he reads what he can of the room. Yasmin is attracted to Fiona. Fiona is harder to read, and Cillian is far more uncomfortable around Fiona than she is around him. London files the information away until there is something useful to do with it.

Yasmin apologizes that she doesn’t have much in the way of accommodations, but she opens two rooms for the crew. Their help may be useful against Jacob and Marcus, who do have mythic abilities at their disposal, and Jacob is power-hungry. So while she doesn’t give them a specific task, the implication is that providing shelter comes with the likelihood of one. 

Maeve asks Yasmin if she knows Cyrus, and expresses concern that helping their friends might get him into trouble. Yasmin says he has his own priorities. If helping had worked against those priorities, he wouldn't have done it. So don’t get on his bad side.

## Sleepover

> **Cillian:** “Girls and Geoffrey in this room, the rest of us in this room. Let's go.”

The residency shuffle takes considerably more counting than it should. London elects to join Maeve, Wren, and Geoffrey. Daniel goes with Cillian, John, Sergei, and Kevin. In that room, Cillian finds a deck of cards. Daniel is interested in the stakes.

In the other room, Geoffrey and Maeve finally have time to catch up.

To Geoffrey, Daniel seems like a different person. He acts as though nothing can touch him, as though no risk is worth worrying about. Maeve asks whether something happened to bring this on. Geoffrey knows Daniel had to do things he didn't want to do while the others were gone, including searching London's apartment. But the problem reaches further back than that.

Daniel has been repressing memories of the glass factory incident, where his powers first manifested. He hasn't wanted to admit that something chose him, or visited him, and they have been living inside that polite fiction ever since. Geoffrey hasn't been able to make him feel safe enough to talk about it. Now, while Geoffrey was away, Daniel has turned completely around. This isn't how he behaves.

Maeve asks whether Geoffrey is okay with it, or whether it is terrifying.

> **Geoffrey:** “I'm barely holding my shit together.”

There isn't an answer for that yet. They keep talking, and Geoffrey eventually asks for the details about Maeve and Cillian. First of all: yay. Way overdue. Now, what happened?

> **Maeve:** “We had a perfectly functional working relationship, applied a little vodka to the situation, and decided to blow it up.”

It was fun. A _lot_ of fun. Geoffrey's concern is whether Cillian was in his normal state of mind. Everything in the Sunken City was dialed up. Was that really him? And does Maeve want it to have been him, or would she rather leave it under the heading of a very strange adventure with a couple of very strange nights?

She doesn't know. Casual is fine, if that’s what it is. She’s good with whatever happens. She is pretty confident the mutual interest was there before, but the Sunken City may have been a factor. There hasn't really been time to process anything. And the fact that his past keeps cropping up bothers her. There is so much he won't tell her.

Geoffrey gently but firmly tells her that Cillian’s business is his business, and that especially if this Thing between them is not an exclusive one, it’s emphatically _not_ hers. She acknowledges the truth in that, though really, getting even casually involved with a chronically curious private investigator is maybe not the best strategy if you have a lot of things you want to keep buried. 

The DA situation worries her too. Geoffrey knows the DA was looking for him. Maeve suspects Armand is using legal channels against everyone he saw at the enclave—which could put Cillian in serious trouble. They need Sofia to tell them who is actually implicated.

Geoffrey recalls Daniel offering David Orlog information and assistance while trying to find the missing crew, even hinting at his abilities. Maeve questions the decision. They piece the contacts back together: David wanted help finding Erica, learned Maeve was missing, and Daniel offered his own services while seeking help. At the river, Armand offered amnesty to Maeve alone, which she rejected. David wasn't at the riverbank. He didn't see Maeve there. Armand could tell him, but they don't know whether that conversation has happened.

London braids Maeve's hair while they talk. He’s unexpectedly good at this, and admits that he used to wear his hair a lot longer.

> **Geoffrey:** “And who are you dating anyway, Wren?”<br />
> **Wren:** “My business.”

Geoffrey pulls Wren into the conversation. She’s very much single; her shop takes all her time. Also, there’s the crew. She feels like she’s dating them, at this point. But the shop is doing a bit better and she has two guys she can trust to run things while she’s gone, which is good, because she’s been gone a lot lately. 

In turn, Wren asks about Inez. Geoffrey says she is staying with a friend. According to Daniel, she seems physically okay, but she isn't telling them much. He doesn't know how much is ordinary resistance, how much is trauma, or whether she is simply too frightened to talk about what happened. Wren points out that all of those things could be true at once.

Inez is twelve and has been with them for a few years. Geoffrey wouldn't change being her parent. He just wishes he had more experience.

## Poker Night
> GalleryMaeve: “I need to get the two of them in a bathroom again. Clearly we need to have a conversation.”

As they ante up, Cillian hesitantly asks John, “Will she actually try to go places, or will she just learn through the internet and shit? Call.”

John picks up his phone. 

“No, call as in poker. Will she, like, go places to research, or will she just find out through the internet or some shit?”

John puts the phone down again. “Yes. She’s good at her job.”

Cillian winces. “Yeah. You're her friend, right?”

“...Yes?”

“Don’t let her go places. If she finds out through the internet, that's fine. But tell her not to go places.”

John responds. “Don't let her go places. All right. Would it be unsafe, or do you want her to not know the answer? Either one's fine.”

“It’s unsafe. If she researches and goes away, that's fine.”

Daniel is watching the exchange. “Oh, that bad? Interesting.”

Cillian glances at Daniel and sighs. “I hope you're not bluffing.”
